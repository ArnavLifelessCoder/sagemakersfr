## 1. Executive Summary

A blocking-plus-classifier pipeline that treats the problem as many-to-one assignment: every Source-2/3 record
is retrieved against the Source-1 entities of its country and state through three sparse TF-IDF channels
(name character 3-grams, canonical address tokens with composite house identifiers, and both together),
filtered by a small LightGBM stage, scored by a LightGBM matcher on about 100 pairwise, lexical and
collective features, and finally attached to its most probable entity with a per-entity decision that
maximises the expected F0.5 (the empty prediction included). The key innovations are label-free
"distractor word" and "sibling group" evidence that transfer to the unseen country (France) and to
native-script vocabulary, a per-country calibration of the decision to the generator's cluster-size prior,
and composite identifier parsing for Indian addresses.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Problem Analysis

Measured on the training data (identical in the US and India):

- Each Source-1 entity has 3.46 true records on average (Source 2: 1.67, Source 3: 1.79); 5.6% are singletons.
  Every Source-2/3 record matches at most one Source-1 entity, so assignment is many-to-one.
- 26% of Source-2/3 records match nothing. They are not random businesses: they are *siblings* of a real
  Source-1 entity (same street, near-identical name) generated by adding or swapping a real vocabulary word
  (holdings, exports, eastgate, groupe, développement, bakery, jewellers in Latin or native script), changing
  the legal form (Private Limited -> Limited, SAS -> SASU) and editing one sub-number of the house identifier
  (25/1923 -> 25/1932, 506 -> 527). A sibling usually has several records that agree with each other.
- True-record noise is different in kind: typos, accents, case, dropped or shuffled words and legal words,
  wrapper junk ("--", "(ID: 72277)", "| www.x.com"), domain names, DBA / "formerly known as" prefixes,
  completely random new names at the same address, native-script transliterations, address abbreviation,
  component reordering, zero-padding, truncated numbers, an added labelled number ("Hn 402"), or an empty
  address (4.5% of true records but only 0.3% of distractors).
- The test set has the same true-record generator but 1.9x the distractor density: matching-state slices
  decompose as 59% true records + 41% distractors (train: 73% / 27%), so probabilities calibrated on training
  odds over-accept on test, and per-country calibration to the prior of about 3.4 predicted matches per
  entity is essential. France is absent from training; 35% of its Source-2/3 addresses end with a city and no
  region, which a city-to-region map learned from Source-1 addresses fills in.
- Indian house identifiers are composite ("4-1-10/1/2", "Plot No-176", "H.no 332", "KH NO. -570/13"); the
  leading-integer house number that works for the US agrees on only 17% of true Indian pairs.

### 2.2 Solution Strategy

**Approach Type:** Blocking + two-stage classifier + per-entity decision (collective features)

**Core Innovation:** label-free distractor evidence (word document-frequency ratios between Source-2/3 and
Source-1 names computed on the data being scored, sibling-group "peer" features, anchor consistency with the
entity's other records) combined with an expected-F0.5 decision per entity calibrated to the cluster-size prior.

## 3. Candidate Generation (Blocking)

Search runs from the Source-2/3 side inside each (country, canonical state) partition; records without a
recognised state are searched country-wide, and French cities are mapped to their region with a map learned
from Source-1 addresses.

- **Blocking keys used:** three TF-IDF channels with exact sparse top-k retrieval (sparse_dot_topn):
  (1) character 3-grams of the normalised core name (legal forms, honorifics, wrappers, domains removed;
  native script transliterated with a table learned from matched training pairs plus a phonetic fallback for
  unseen words); (2) canonical address tokens (abbreviations, ordinals, drop-words) plus a composite
  `HS_<housenumber>_<street>` token and `ID_<identifier>` tokens for composite house identifiers;
  (3) the concatenation of (1) and (2). Top-20 per channel, then the union of the top-10 by each channel's
  cosine and by their sum; 40 by name for records without a state.
- **Candidate pairs generated:** about 19 per Source-2/3 record after blocking; the stage-1 LightGBM filter
  (cheap vectorised string similarities and rank/gap context, threshold keeping 99.95% of true pairs in
  training) leaves about 1.0 per record. These stage-1 survivors are exactly the pairs scored by the final
  model and are what `candidate_pairs.tsv` contains (about 11 million pairs on the test set).
- **How you ensured true matches were not lost:** recall measured on labelled training slices: 99.68% on a
  9-state slice and 99.2% on a 22-state 30% slice that includes Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Texas and Ohio (98.1% before the third channel and the wider keep); the
  remaining misses are mostly empty-address records with common names (irreducible name-only ambiguity) and
  random renames with a partial address.

## 4. Matching Model

**Features used:**
- Name features: rapidfuzz ratio / token-sort / token-set / partial / Jaro-Winkler on core names and on
  concatenated names, exact and fuzzy extra/missing token counts, first-token equality, subset relations,
  legal-form agreement / conflict / one-sided counts, DBA-alternative-name similarity, native-script and
  domain flags.
- Address features: address-token and street/city similarities, house-number relation category
  (equal, suffix, truncated, small / mid / large difference) and log difference, number-set jaccard and
  one-sided counts, composite identifier jaccard, edit distance, replaced-vs-added identifier counts,
  state agreement, empty-address flag, "1/2" flag.
- Other: (a) token lexicon: out-of-fold log-odds of each extra / missing token (and `L:<legal>` tokens)
  appearing in true vs false near-duplicate pairs, with 40% of distractor entries randomly hidden during
  training so the model also learns from label-free evidence; (b) label-free vocabulary and modifier
  features: document frequency of the differing tokens among Source-1 names and the Source-2/3 vs Source-1
  frequency ratio, computed per country on the data being scored (this is what transfers to France and to
  native-script business words); (c) stage-1 probability and its rank / gap context per record and per
  entity; (d) anchor features comparing the record with the entity's best other record (house number and
  number-set agreement, name similarity); (e) peer features counting other candidates of the same entity
  that share the record's extra word or new number (sibling groups).

**Model type:** LightGBM (binary, 127 leaves, learning rate 0.05, early stopping on held-out entities);
stage 1 is a smaller LightGBM on the cheap features. Trained on a stratified 30% slice of Source-1 entities
per country (whole states, so distractor density is realistic) with all their true records and the unmatched
records of the same states; 20% of entities held out by hash for validation and threshold selection.

**Optional neural component (used in the blended submissions):** a cross-encoder (`xlm-roberta-base`, MIT
licence, 278M parameters) fine-tuned for one epoch on 1.5M stage-1 candidate pairs of the training slice
(text = "name | address | country" of both records; negatives capped at three per positive, hardest first;
`src/cross_encoder.py`, `src/export_pairs.py`). Its probability is blended with the LightGBM probability in
logit space with weight 0.3 (`src/blend_ce.py`); on the 135k validation entities this lifts F0.5 from 0.9906
to 0.9918. The pretrained weights are a model (no external data lookup); training and scoring of the 12.2M
test pairs took about 1.5 hours on one L40S GPU.

**Ensembling and decision calibration (final submissions):** two LightGBM runs trained on disjoint stratified
30% slices (seeds 0 and 1; the second adds neighbour-state search for Telangana/Andhra Pradesh and DC/Washington
records and label-free name and address frequency features) are averaged per pair (`src/ens_ckpt.py`), blended
with the cross-encoder, and decided per entity. Because the test set carries 1.9x the training distractor density
and they concentrate in the middle probability bands, each probability is first divided by the inflation of its
band on the test relative to the validation slice (records per entity per band, computed label-free; the top band
is trusted), then the expected-F0.5 rule keeps the best prefix per entity (`src/assemble.py --band-ref`).

**Threshold selection method:** each record goes to its most probable entity; a transductive per-country lexicon (words that separate the run's own confident matches from its confident rejections, training lexicon kept for known words) can rescore a country without labels, which is how the French sibling vocabulary (holding, participations, développement, groupe, parents, foyer, loisirs) is learned; per entity, the prefix of its
records (by probability) that maximises the expected F0.5 under independent truths is kept, the empty
prediction included (a 2% allowance for records lost in blocking). On the test set a country whose predicted
matches per entity exceed the validation rate (3.41) by more than 2% has its probabilities shrunk
(odds divided by a constant found by bisection) until it meets the rate, because the test set carries 1.9x
more distractors than training.

## 5. Results & Error Analysis

- **F_0.5 Score (macro):** validation 0.9924 on the 9-state development slice (0.9917 with false merges
  weighted 1.9x to emulate the test distractor density); validation 0.9906 (0.9900 test-like) on a 30% slice of the training entities with 22 states including Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Texas and Ohio (135,052 held-out entities), where the previous version scored 0.9858; public leaderboard: see the submission notes.
- **Common false positives (wrong merges):** a sibling whose only differences are a legal-form change plus a
  new sub-number; an identical name at a far house number when the entity has no other confident record;
  ambiguous empty-address records whose name belongs to several entities in the country.
- **Common false negatives (missed matches):** completely random renames whose address is also partial;
  added generic words ("Enterprises", "Services") on records with an empty address; sub-number edits that
  also occur in true records; records lost in blocking (empty address plus a common name).

---

## 6. Conclusion

The generator behind the data produces two distinguishable kinds of difference from a Source-1 record:
true-record noise and sibling distractors. Encoding that distinction with label-free, per-country statistics
and collective (per-entity) evidence, and deciding per entity with the metric's own expectation, is what moves
the score; the biggest remaining losses are irreducible ambiguities (empty addresses with common names,
random renames with partial addresses).
